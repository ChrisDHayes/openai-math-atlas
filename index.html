Start here · A three-minute evidence briefing

An extraordinary release. An unfinished scientific assessment.

On 6 October 2026, OpenAI released hundreds of mathematical manuscripts from a proprietary frontier model. Some papers propose advances on difficult open problems; some include machine-checkable proofs. But publishing a proof-like artifact is not the same as independent confirmation or scientific acceptance. This distinction is the central question behind the map. OpenAI announcement [1] ↗

✓ What we can establish from the record

Publicly documented facts

  • A real research corpus exists: the current repository reports 719 manuscripts, grouped into 372 families rather than 719 necessarily independent discoveries.
  • Some results have formal artifacts: OpenAI reports 300/719 (~42%) top-line results formalized in Lean; the figure describes documented coverage, not an independently audited correctness rate.
  • The creation process is partially described: roughly 4,000 problems attempted, around three hours of ChatGPT Pro-equivalent compute per result on average, and 10 published reasoning summaries.
  • The record includes corrections: three withdrawals and 14 substantive or documentary revisions are visible in the dated history.
Documentary sources: OpenAI catalogue [2] · revision history [3]
? What has not been established across the corpus

Open questions—not negative verdicts

  • Correctness: that every manuscript—or any specified proportion beyond independently checked cases—contains a valid proof.
  • Correspondence: that each Lean theorem captures precisely the natural-language claim, including all necessary assumptions and dependencies.
  • Novelty and importance: that the proposed results are all genuinely new, consequential or useful for further research.
  • Reproducibility: full independent reproduction of the model generation process, since the internal model is unreleased and the disclosures are selective.
  • Community acceptance: AGMAI's advisory role is explicitly not an endorsement; independent scholarly scrutiny and human understanding are ongoing.
Scientific and governance context: AGMAI response [4] · responsible-release recommendations [5]
ManuscriptAn individual published paper or supporting argument. A manuscript count does not equal a count of confirmed breakthroughs.
Research familyA group of related manuscripts concerning a main result, consequences, alternative proofs or dependencies.

Why this matters beyond mathematics

AI can accelerate answers faster than organizations establish what to trust.

A mathematically correct-looking artifact is not necessarily a meaningful discovery; similarly, an AI-generated forecast, software change or business recommendation can appear complete before its assumptions, context and consequences are understood. The lesson is not to distrust every output, but to design verification and human judgment into the work system. This is Hayes's applied interpretation—not a claim made by the source papers.

A balanced reading of the release

What was claimed, and what is still being examined?

Distinguishing disclosure, logical verification, mathematical correctness, scientific significance and human understanding is essential.

OpenAI's stated case

A broad body of machine-generated research

OpenAI reports results across a range of open mathematics problems, publishes manuscripts and proof artifacts, provides some Lean formalizations, and commits to preserving correction history.

Source: OpenAI's October 6 announcement and the math repository. Announcement ↗
Verification gap

Formalization is not blanket certification

Approximately 42% of top-line results have associated formalizations; some other results may contain errors. A formal proof must also match the claim it purports to establish.

Source: repository README and formalization catalogue. Repository ↗
October 7 corrections

Errors can propagate between papers

OpenAI's history records three withdrawn manuscripts following a sign error, 14 revised manuscripts and 13 other citation updates. Corrections are evidence of scrutiny, not proof that all remaining papers are correct.

Source: OpenAI revision history ↗
Mathematical community

Publication begins—not completes—knowledge formation

AGMAI states that its advisory participation was not an endorsement and calls for genuine human understanding, research freedom and equitable access to strong AI research tools.

Source: AGMAI October 6 statement ↗
Central thesis: generating mathematical manuscripts is not equivalent to independently verifying, interpreting, or incorporating them into enduring mathematical knowledge.
Transparency and attribution

How to interpret this visualization

Source-of-truth and refresh behaviour

V4 coverage: each of the 719 manuscript records receives a source-linked “Why it matters” explanation derived conditionally from its own catalogue abstract, research family and field. Ten highlighted families receive individually authored context. These interpretations are editorial, not 719 independent technical peer reviews. Once all 719 records load and pass integrity checks, use Save complete offline copy to embed the catalogue in a portable HTML file.

The visualization attempts to load the current public CONTENTS.md catalogue from GitHub on each opening. Every paper shown after successful loading is parsed from an actual named manuscript with an associated source PDF path and catalogue summary. If external requests are blocked, the interface visibly reports the limitation and allows a local import of that exact file.

Visual encodings

  • Discipline colours group related manuscripts by subject. Sector angle corresponds to the number of result families, using exact catalogue section boundaries (not significance or correctness). The correct family counts include 39 in algebraic and complex geometry, 41 in theoretical computer science, and 11 in partial differential equations.
  • Branch length (V4.3): in the default Variable mode, each manuscript spoke's length is proportional to the logarithm of its research family's manuscript count (length = 22 + 96 × ln n / ln nmax units), so single-paper families stay visible while large families stand out. Dashed radial guides mark 1, 2, 5, 10… manuscripts. Length encodes family size only—not importance, novelty or validity. Choose Uniform to restore the original equal-length spokes.
  • The middle annulus contains one mark for each of the 372 families. The outer periphery contains one individual mark per paper loaded from the catalogue.
  • The family-to-discipline assignment is based on the field sequence and family counts in OpenAI's overview.tex. This is a catalogue taxonomy, not a statistical clustering or similarity model.
  • Individual paper titles and abstracts are publisher-authored descriptions of claimed results. They have not all been independently corroborated.
  • An individual manuscript is marked 'in Lean source index' only when its catalogue PDF path exactly matches an item under sources: in OpenAI’s formalization.yaml. That does not mean the verification was independently rerun here or that the formal statement matches the prose claim. The manuscript-level exact-match tally is distinct from OpenAI’s 300/719 figure for formalized top-line results.

Notes on the figures

719 manuscripts, 372 result families and approximately 42% top-line formalization coverage are reported in the repository as of October 11, 2026. OpenAI reports roughly 4,000 problems attempted and an average of around three hours' equivalent ChatGPT Pro thinking compute per result. These measures do not constitute an independently audited success rate.
